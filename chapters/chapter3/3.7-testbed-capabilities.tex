\section{Testbed Capabilities}
\label{chap:proposal:capabilities}

The preceding sections defined the capabilities the proposed testbed should offer to verticals, organized into resource lifecycle management, network exposure, network observability and service delivery. This section consolidates these capabilities and maps them to the \acs{PoC} functional and non-functional requirements gathered in Section~\ref{chap:proposal:use-case:requirements}, thereby illustrating how the testbed proposed capabilities support the practical needs of real-world vertical use cases. Table~\ref{tab:proposal:capabilities} summarizes this mapping.

As established in Section~\ref{chap:proposal:delivery}, at the top level, the testbed should provide a single management and automation platform (an \acs{OSS}) through which experimenters can order and manage all testbed functionality, exposed as services, via \acs{TMF} Open \acsp{API}. These services comprise the network slices and \acsp{UE} supporting the experiment, the exposure \acsp{API}, the monitoring services, and the network application itself. The platform would then delegate each service order to the appropriate orchestration and management platforms, following the dependency chain defined in that same section. In particular, the orchestration of the corresponding workloads, either as containerized or virtualized resources, would in turn fulfill the portability and deployment constraint imposed by \ref{enum:nfr:packaging}.

Initially, the testbed should provision the network slices that establish the connectivity conditions for the collision detection service, following the management hierarchy described in Section~\ref{chap:proposal:resource_lifecycle}, composed of the \acs{CSMF}, \acs{NSMF} and \acs{NSSMF}. This approach reserves the resources required by the \acs{PoC} before its operation, according to the \acs{QoS} requirements specified by the experimenter, i.e. the uplink requirements to support the camera video stream. Once the slice is deployed, the roadside camera \acs{UE} would be associated with it, so that its traffic is subject to the \acs{QoS} constraints defined for that slice. In doing so, the testbed addresses the application's requirement for dedicated and isolated network resources (\ref{enum:fr:slice}), theoretically guaranteeing the uplink bitrate required to sustain the live video stream from the camera to the inference server (\ref{enum:fr:stream}, \ref{enum:nfr:uplink-bitrate}).

Once the full experiment is provisioned, the application can take advantage of the network's capabilities through the exposure layer described in Section~\ref{chap:proposal:exposure}. These capabilities should be offered at two abstraction levels, by the \acs{NEF} and CAMARA \acsp{API}, and accessed through a single framework responsible for their secure publication, discovery and invocation, i.e. \acs{CAPIF}. This relieves application developers from the burden of interacting with distinct authentication servers for each exposure component, providing a unified approach to authentication and authorization (\ref{enum:nfr:secure-exposure}).

The requirements concerning the camera's data session would be fulfilled by the \acs{QoS} configuration and analytics capabilities of the network exposure layer. The provisioned slice and \acs{UE} association establish the baseline, reserving the required resources and defining the default \acs{QoS}. On top of this baseline, the \acs{NEF} \textit{AsSessionWithQoS} and the CAMARA \acs{QoD} \acsp{API} can be used to adjust the \acs{QoS} at runtime. This distinction is relevant for the \acs{PoC}, since the network conditions experienced by the video stream may degrade over time, compromising the frames delivered to the inference server and, consequently, the detection of collisions, which may warrant a change of \acs{QoS}.

Through these \acsp{API}, the application would reference the \acs{QoS} profile of the camera's session (\ref{enum:fr:qos-profile}) and request the uplink throughput and latency required for the streaming use case (\ref{enum:fr:qos-request}). In parallel, it would subscribe to network analytics to monitor the network conditions of the streaming session (\ref{enum:fr:monitoring}), e.g. throughput. Upon a violation of the thresholds of the monitoring profile, i.e. the expected network conditions for the streaming session, the application would switch the active \acs{QoS} profile of the camera \acs{UE} to a higher-priority \acs{QoS} profile (\ref{enum:fr:qos-change}). As this adaptation is bounded by the time limits imposed on degradation detection (\ref{enum:nfr:degradation-detection}) and \acs{QoS} enforcement (\ref{enum:nfr:qos-enforcement}), both requirements would depend directly on the response/callback times of the exposure interfaces.

To detect such changing network conditions, the \acs{NEF} \textit{AnalyticsExposure} and CAMARA \textit{Connectivity Insights Subscription} \acsp{API} would be used. In addition, when using the CAMARA alternative the \textit{Application Profiles} \acs{API} can also be used to define the network quality thresholds, i.e. the expected minimum uplink throughput for the streaming session (\ref{enum:fr:monitoring-profile}). When relying on the \acs{NEF}, these thresholds would instead be maintained by the application itself. These \acsp{API} should be properly integrated with the testbed's observation layer, as described in Section~\ref{chap:proposal:observability}, namely with its passive monitoring mechanisms which should monitor/capture the traffic of the video stream, between the camera, i.e. the \acs{UE}, and the inference server, i.e. the application server. Active monitoring (probing) capabilities can be used as well to evaluate the latency of the \acs{UE}'s \acs{5G} connection, separately from the application traffic.

The requirements concerning road users are expected to be fulfilled by the testbed's device location and notification capabilities. Specifically, \acs{UE} location retrieval, provided through the \acs{NEF} MonitoringEvent or the CAMARA Device Location \acsp{API}, would allow the application to track the vehicles and pedestrians in the vicinity of the monitored road segment and display their positions on the operator dashboard (\ref{enum:fr:ue-tracking}), without requiring any instrumentation on their devices. Through the same \acsp{API}, geofencing subscriptions would in turn notify the application whenever a \acs{UE} enters or leaves the predefined area surrounding the monitored road segment, while location verification would allow it to check on demand whether a given \acs{UE} lies within that area (\ref{enum:fr:geofencing}).

However, alerting these road users, when a collision is detected, requires notification mechanisms not covered by the preceding sections. The testbed should therefore provide mechanisms to notify pedestrian and vehicle \acsp{UE} via unicast, e.g. \acs{SMS} addressed by \acs{MSISDN}, or multicast, e.g. a broadcast to all \acsp{UE} of a given class within a geographical area, periodically reissuing these alerts until the incident is deemed resolved (\ref{enum:fr:notify-pedestrians}, \ref{enum:fr:notify-vehicles}, \ref{enum:fr:notify-periodic}, \ref{enum:nfr:notification-time}). Although motivated by the \acs{PoC}, these mechanisms are not specific to it and would remain available to other applications.

The remaining requirements, namely the collision detection performed by the inference server (\ref{enum:fr:detection}, \ref{enum:nfr:frame-processing}), its reporting to the application backend (\ref{enum:fr:detection-event}) and the operator dashboard (\ref{enum:fr:dashboard}, \ref{enum:fr:dashboard-collisions}), concern the internal logic of the application and are not mapped to any testbed capability. The same could be said about the logging of detection events and network \acs{API} interactions (\ref{enum:nfr:logging}), which is primarily a responsibility of the application. Nevertheless, the testbed may also contribute to it by internally logging the operations resulting from these interactions, such as the enforcement of a given \acs{QoS} profile, and by displaying them alongside the collected metrics in its monitoring dashboards.
