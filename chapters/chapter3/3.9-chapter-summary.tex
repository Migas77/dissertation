\section{Chapter Summary}
\label{chap:proposal:summary}

This chapter described the testbed proposed in this dissertation, from its overall design to the procedure through which it should be validated. Each component was motivated by the needs of vertical experimenters and, where applicable, aligned with the relevant \acs{3GPP}, \acs{TMF}, \acs{GSMA} and CAMARA specifications.

After presenting the proposed architecture in Section~\ref{chap:proposal:architecture}, the chapter addressed the lifecycle management of network slices and their \acsp{UE} in Section~\ref{chap:proposal:resource_lifecycle}. Based on the \acs{3GPP} network slice management model, the proposed testbed can manage all phases of the \acs{NSI} lifecycle by delegating the slice requirements captured by the \acs{OSS}, acting as the \acs{CSMF}, to the \acs{NSMF} and \acs{NSSMF} functions, utilizing an \acs{NFVO} for the orchestration of virtualized network functions. The section also described the configuration required across the \acsp{NF} of the \acs{5GC} to associate a \acs{UE} with a target slice.

Regarding network exposure, Section~\ref{chap:proposal:exposure} considered three \acs{NEF} northbound \acsp{API} crucial for the development of novel network applications. Among these, the \textit{AsSessionWithQoS} and \textit{AnalyticsExposure} \acsp{API} were discussed in detail, covering their respective data models and information flows. These \acsp{API} enable applications to request specific \acs{QoS} requirements for their traffic flows and retrieve analytics on observed network conditions, respectively. Similar capabilities were also explored under the CAMARA initiative, whose \acsp{API} offer simpler, developer-facing interfaces at the cost of reduced control over the network. Access to both sets of \acsp{API} is then consolidated under \acs{CAPIF}, which governs their secure publication, discovery and invocation by network applications.

Section~\ref{chap:proposal:observability} addressed network observability, identifying the metrics most relevant to the testbed and their sources in the \acs{5G} System. In this section, it was emphasized the need for a monitoring process that operates autonomously within the testbed, i.e. passive monitoring. Active monitoring was also proposed to collect metrics such as \acs{RTT}, jitter and packet loss through synthetic network traffic. The collected metrics should be delivered through the \acs{NEF} and CAMARA analytics \acsp{API}, as well as visual dashboards.

Next, Section~\ref{chap:proposal:delivery} described how the testbed functionality should be offered as services through the \acs{OSS}, by following the \acs{TMF} \acs{ODA} and its Open \acsp{API}. Based on this, the section presented the list of services required for the delivery of complete experiments, covering the network slice and its \acsp{UE}, the exposure interfaces, the monitoring stack and, possibly, the network application onboarded by the experimenter. For the delivery of \acs{5G} resources, the specifications for these services were proposed based on standardized templates and specifications. These include the \acs{GSMA} \acs{GST}, through which verticals can express their slice requirements with a standardized set of attributes. In the absence of an equivalent standardized template for \acsp{UE}, a minimal blueprint was proposed for their modeling based on available \acs{3GPP} specifications. The section also outlined how the entire lifecycle of an experiment should be managed in alignment with the \acs{TMF} Open \acsp{API}, to order and orchestrate its services sequentially, according to their dependencies.

To demonstrate these capabilities, Section~\ref{chap:proposal:use-case} introduced a multi-vertical use case targeting the \acs{PPDR} and automotive sectors, through a vehicle collision detection and notification solution. The functional and non-functional requirements of this application were identified and then mapped to the proposed testbed capabilities, which were summarized in Section~\ref{chap:proposal:capabilities}. 

Lastly, Section~\ref{chap:proposal:validation} proposed a methodology for validating the proposed network application and, consequently, the capabilities and network interfaces of the testbed. Two complementary approaches were considered to reproduce varying network conditions, (i)~emulating network responses at the exposure layer and (ii)~exercising the application under real network conditions in a deployed \acs{5G} network. The validation covers the service delivery process, verifying that a complete experiment is consistently provisioned through a single service order, and the multi-vertical use case, focusing on the adaptive \acs{QoS} loop exercised through both the \acs{NEF} and CAMARA \acsp{API}.
